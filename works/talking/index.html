Talking

Stand in front of the mirror. The room stays yours. So do your clothes, your hands, the way you move. Only the face, slowly, turns into mine.

What it is

It isn't a filter pasted on top. Every frame is repainted by a model that has learned one face.

You walk up to a screen and see yourself. Then something starts to drift. As you turn your head, the face morphs and glitches, because a model is repainting the whole picture many times a second, and the only face it has ever learned is mine.

It's about what it feels like to have your face borrowed. And what it feels like to borrow someone else's body.

How it works

camera → TouchDesigner → Stable Diffusion, img2img → TouchDesigner → screen + a fast LoRA (1–2 steps) + my face LoRA
  1. Photos. About fifty ordinary photos of me, in different places, light and clothes.
  2. Captions. Each photo is labelled with only what changes between them. The things that make me look like me, the glasses and the curls, are left out on purpose, so the model ties them to my name.
  3. Training. A small add-on that teaches the image model one face, trained in about fifteen minutes on a rented GPU.
  4. Live. Each camera frame is repainted gently: enough for the face to become mine, not so much that the room and your pose are lost. A speed-up trick gets that down to one or two steps a frame, which is what makes it run live on a laptop.

There's also a room version: instead of selfies, the model is trained on full frames of the room the piece stands in, from the same camera. It learns the space as well as the face, so the colours of the room stay true.

Credit

This is a learning re-build of Surrogate Bodies by Aisling Phelan, an installation where visitors wear the artist's AI-generated face, which she presented at the TouchDesigner Event in Dublin in June 2025. The idea and the pipeline are hers. I rebuilt it from her talk, with my own face, and changed it to run on a Mac instead of a gaming PC.

The code is open. It doesn't include my photos or my face model, and it asks one thing: only train it on your own face, or on someone who has said yes.
