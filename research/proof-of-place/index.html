Proof of Place

A photo says where it was taken, and when. High above, a satellite saw the same ground that week. I make the two of them compare stories.

The scene

Liveness checks ask "is this a real face?" Nobody asks "is this a real field?"

In India, a lot of public money moves because of a single geotagged photo. A worksite for MGNREGA. A house for PMAY. A ruined crop for an insurance claim. The officials who check them already know the tricks: a photo of a photo, a picture from last season passed off as this one. The location stamp is easy to fake. The date is easier.

My day job is liveness: making sure the face in front of a camera belongs to a real person, standing there, right now. Proof of Place asks the same question of a place.

The second witness

The European Sentinel satellites photograph every field in India every few days. Their cameras see how green a crop is. Their radar sees through monsoon cloud, and it sees water and change. So any photo that claims a place and a date has a second witness: an independent record of what that ground looked like that week.

  • Crop stageDoes the greenness on that date match what the photo shows?
  • WaterWas the field flooded, or dry, the way the photo says?
  • ChangeDid something actually happen between before and after?

Every claim gets a verdict, the check it failed, and a one-page evidence card a person can read in thirty seconds. And when the witness didn't see clearly, because of cloud, missing radar or a plot too small to make out, it says "can't tell". It doesn't guess. A tool like this is only worth anything if it admits what it doesn't know.

The first evidence card

What nobody has tried

Earlier work checks whether a photo's hour and month make sense for where it claims to be, or compares it with a fixed map image. As far as I can find, nobody checks a ground photo against what the satellite saw of that exact field on that exact date. And nobody treats it as an attack to defend against, with a list of tricks (the wrong date, a shifted GPS pin, the neighbour's field, a photo of a screen, an AI edit) and an error rate for each one.

What it can't do, plainly: a satellite pixel is 10 metres wide, so very small plots are out of reach. It catches the wrong place, the wrong time or the wrong state of the land. It won't catch a fake photo of the right scene.

No results yet beyond single evidence cards. When there are, they will be here.
