Proof of Place

A photo says where and when it was taken. Satellites were watching that spot too. So I check the photo against them.

The problem

Liveness checks ask "is this a real face?" Nobody asks "is this a real field?"

A lot of public money in India moves on the strength of a geotagged photo. A worksite photo for MGNREGA, a house for PMAY, a damaged crop for an insurance claim. Officials already complain about photos of photos and pictures reused from last season. The location stamp is easy to fake, and the date is easier.

I spend my day job on liveness: checking that the face in front of a camera is a real person, here, now. Proof of Place asks the same question of a place.

How it checks

The European Sentinel satellites photograph every field in India every few days. Optical images show how green a crop is. Radar sees through monsoon cloud and shows water and change. So for any photo that claims a place and a date, there is an independent record of what that place looked like that week.

  • Crop stageDoes the greenness on that date match what the photo shows?
  • WaterWas the field flooded, or dry, the way the photo says?
  • ChangeDid something actually happen between before and after?

Each claim gets a verdict, the check that failed, and a one-page evidence card a person can read in thirty seconds. When cloud, missing radar or a tiny plot leave too little evidence, it says "can't tell" instead of guessing. A tool like this is only useful if it is honest about that.

The first evidence card

What's new about it

Earlier work checks whether a photo's hour and month fit its location, or compares it with a fixed map image. As far as I can find, nobody checks a ground photo against the satellite state of that exact field on the claimed date. Nobody treats it as an attack-detection problem either, with a list of attacks (wrong date, shifted GPS, the neighbour's field, a photo of a screen, an AI edit) and error rates reported for each one.

What it can't do, said plainly: satellite pixels are 10 metres wide, so very small plots are out of reach. It catches the wrong place, the wrong time or the wrong state of the land. It does not catch a fake photo of the right scene.

No results yet beyond single evidence cards. I will put them here when there are some.
